I'm Learning

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 9 September 2026

I'm Learning does not collect any data about you. It has no accounts, no servers, and no internet access. Everything you put into it stays on your device.

The sections below say the same thing in more detail, and are honest about the one place your words touch software this app does not control.

What the app keeps

I'm Learning stores the material you create so it can teach it back to you:

  • the languages you have added
  • the cards you have made and their names
  • the words and translations you have entered
  • your progress for each word — its level, its streak, and when it is next due
  • your preferences, such as the interface language and whether the dark theme is on

All of it is held on your device and nowhere else. None of it is sent to the developer or to anyone else, because the app has no way to send anything.

What the app does not do

These are not promises about intent. They are properties of how the app is built, and you can check them against the app's listing on Google Play:

  • No internet access. The published app does not request the INTERNET permission, so it cannot transmit anything, to anyone, ever.
  • No permissions at all. It asks for no access to your location, camera, microphone, contacts, files, or phone.
  • No accounts and no sign-in. You are never asked for a name, an email address, or a password.
  • No advertising. There are no ads and no advertising software of any kind in the app.
  • No analytics or tracking. No usage statistics, no crash reporting, no cookies, no advertising identifier, no device identifiers of any kind.

Reading words aloud

The app can pronounce a word for you. When you ask it to, it hands that single word to the text-to-speech engine already installed on your device — a separate piece of software, usually supplied by the maker of your phone or by Google.

The app sends nothing else and receives nothing back but the sound. Whether that engine speaks entirely on your device or uses its own internet connection to do it is the engine's behaviour, governed by its own privacy policy and by your device's settings, not by this app. If you would rather no word ever left the app, do not use the speaker button.

Export and import

The app can write everything you have — words, cards, and progress — into a block of text, so you can move it to another device. That text is handed to you and to nobody else. Where you then put it is entirely your decision, and once it leaves the app this policy no longer covers it. Importing works the same way in reverse: the app reads only what you paste into it.

Children

I'm Learning is suitable for children. It collects no personal information from anyone, of any age, and has no means of transmitting any, so there is nothing to gather, share, or sell.

It contains no advertising, no in-app purchases, no chat, no accounts, no links out to other apps or websites, and no way for one user to see or contact another. Words a child types are visible only on that child's own device.

Because no personal information is collected, the app does not knowingly collect information from children under 13, and there is nothing for a parent to request access to or deletion of beyond deleting the app itself. This is intended to satisfy the United States Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and the Google Play Families policy.

Your data and your rights

Rights such as access, correction, portability, and erasure exist to give you power over data somebody else is holding. Nobody is holding any of yours:

  • Access and portability — everything is already on your device, and Export hands you the whole of it in readable form.
  • Correction — you can edit or delete any word, card, or language from within the app.
  • Erasure — uninstalling the app removes everything it stored. There is no copy anywhere else to ask about.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever gains a feature that changes any of the above, this page will be updated before that version is published, and the date at the top will change with it.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 October 2026

I'm Learning does not collect any data about you. It has no accounts, no servers, and no internet access. The app sends nothing to anyone: what you put into it is kept on your device.

The sections below say the same thing in more detail, and are honest about the two places your cards touch software this app does not control: your device's speech engine, and your device's own backup.

  • No account
  • No internet access
  • No ads
  • No tracking

On your device

What the app keeps

I'm Learning stores what you create so it can teach it back to you:

  • the languages you have added
  • your cards — each term, its translation, and any note you gave it
  • each card's progress — how well you have recalled it and when you last answered it, which is what decides the cards you are shown each day
  • the days you practised, which the streak and the calendar are drawn from
  • your preferences, such as the interface language, the dark theme, the reminder's time and how the games are set up
  • recordings of your cards' terms, made by your device's speech engine so a card can be read aloud without a wait. They are held in the app's cache, and Cached audio in Settings shows their size and clears them.

All of it is held by the app on your device. None of it is sent to the developer or to anyone else, because the app has no way to send anything.

What the app does not do

These are not promises about intent. They are properties of how the app is built, and you can check them against the app's listing on Google Play:

  • No internet access. The published app does not request the INTERNET permission, so it cannot transmit anything, to anyone, ever.
  • No access to your personal data. It asks for no access to your location, camera, microphone, contacts, storage, or phone. Saving and opening a file uses your device's own file picker, which hands the app the one file you chose and nothing else — no permission is asked for, and none is held afterwards. The only permissions the app holds are the ones its daily reminder needs, described below.
  • No accounts and no sign-in. You are never asked for a name, an email address, or a password.
  • No advertising. There are no ads and no advertising software of any kind in the app.
  • No analytics or tracking. No usage statistics, no crash reporting, no cookies, no advertising identifier, no device identifiers of any kind.

Where other software is involved

Reading cards aloud

For a language your device has a voice for, the app can read a card's term aloud. It does so through the text-to-speech engine already installed on your device — a separate piece of software, usually supplied by the maker of your phone or by Google.

The engine is handed the term — never the translation, never the note — and nothing else. That happens when a card is added, edited or imported, so a recording of it can be made and kept, and again only if that recording is missing when the card is to be read. A card is read as it arrives in front of you, if Auto pronounce is on in Settings, and whenever you tap its speaker; both normally just play the recording.

The app receives nothing back but the sound. Whether that engine speaks entirely on your device or uses its own internet connection to do it is the engine's behaviour, governed by its own privacy policy and by your device's settings, not by this app. Turning Auto pronounce off stops cards being read as they arrive; it does not stop the recordings being made. A language your device has no voice for is never handed to the engine at all.

The daily reminder

The reminder is off unless you turn it on in the app's Settings, where you also choose its time — 13:00 until you pick another. When it is on and you have not finished the day's cards by then, the app shows a notification reminding you to practise. It is decided and scheduled entirely on your device: nothing is sent anywhere to arrange it, and its text is fixed — it never contains your cards.

Turning it on is the only time the app asks for permission to show notifications. It also holds two further permissions that Android grants without asking: one to learn that the phone has restarted, so the reminder can be set again, and one to vibrate when it arrives. If you refuse the notification permission the reminder simply stays off and the app works exactly as before; it can be turned off again at any time.

Export and import

The app can write your whole collection — languages, cards and progress, along with the days you practised — into a single file, so you can keep a copy or move it to another device. It asks your device's own file picker where to put it, and the file goes only where you say. It is handed to you and to nobody else; where you keep it afterwards is entirely your decision, and once it leaves the app this policy no longer covers it.

Importing works the same way in reverse: the picker asks you for one file — a backup, or a list of cards as CSV or plain text — and the app reads only that one. What the picker hands over is a temporary copy, which the app discards once it has been read.

Your device's own backup

Android can back up the data of the apps on your phone to your own Google account, and can copy it across when you set up a new phone. If you have that switched on in your device's settings, the system may include what this app has saved — your languages, cards, progress and preferences — as it does for most apps. The recordings are not included.

That copy is made by the operating system, not by this app, which still sends nothing itself. It goes to your account and nobody else's; the developer cannot see it. It is governed by Google's privacy policy and by your device's backup settings, which is also where it is switched off.

You and this policy

Children

I'm Learning is suitable for children. It collects no personal information from anyone, of any age, and has no means of transmitting any, so there is nothing to gather, share, or sell.

It contains no advertising, no in-app purchases, no chat, no accounts, no links out to other apps or websites, and no way for one user to see or contact another. Cards a child writes are visible only on that child's own device.

Because no personal information is collected, the app does not knowingly collect information from children under 13, and there is nothing for a parent to request access to or deletion of beyond deleting the app itself. This is intended to satisfy the United States Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and the Google Play Families policy.

Your data and your rights

Rights such as access, correction, portability, and erasure exist to give you power over data somebody else is holding. Nobody is holding any of yours:

  • Access and portability — everything is already on your device, and Export hands you the whole collection in readable form.
  • Correction — you can edit or delete any card or language from within the app.
  • Erasure — uninstalling the app removes everything it stored on the device. The only copies elsewhere are ones you made yourself: a file you exported, or your device's own backup if you have it switched on.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever gains a feature that changes any of the above, this page will be updated before that version is published, and the date at the top will change with it.
